
# Lab Proof: First Match ≠ Right Match

## 1. What I built

I built a Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) system using Pinecone for vector search and a CrossEncoder for reranking retrieved documents.

The system retrieves relevant information from the EU AI Act and other source documents, generates answers using an LLM, and provides citations.

I compared baseline retrieval with CrossEncoder reranking to evaluate answer accuracy, retrieval relevance, and source grounding.

**Notebook:** [Relevance Scoring and Rerankers](relevance_scoring_rerankers.ipynb)

## 6. Baseline vs. Reranked

**Test query:** What fines apply for using a prohibited AI practice?

### Retrieval Comparison

| Evaluation | Baseline | Reranked |
|---|---|---|
| Relevant chunk | regulation-665 | regulation-665 |
| Source page | 115 | 115 |
| Position of relevant chunk | Rank 3 | Rank 1 |
| Answer correctness | Correct | Correct |
| Citation | EU AI Act, p.115 | EU AI Act, p.115 |

### What Changed?

Both retrieval methods generated the same correct answer: prohibited AI practices may result in administrative fines of up to EUR 35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher.

However, CrossEncoder reranking moved the relevant evidence from third position to first position.

This demonstrates an improvement in retrieval ranking, even though the generated answer remained unchanged.

### Was Reranking Worth the Cost?

In the earlier Q1 timing test:

- Baseline response time: 4.09 seconds
- Reranked response time: 4.39 seconds
- Additional time: 0.30 seconds

Reranking introduced an additional model-scoring step.

For Q4, the main benefit was improved evidence ranking rather than answer accuracy.

**Conclusion:** Reranking was beneficial for prioritizing relevant evidence, but the Q4 answer itself did not improve. Across the five evaluation questions, reranking showed additional benefits in answer accuracy for Q2 and Q5. More repeated tests are needed to confirm whether these improvements justify the additional computational cost.

## 7. Failure or Limitation

### Incorrect or Unsupported Citations

One limitation identified during testing was that the RAG system sometimes generated answers with citations that did not fully support its claims.

For example, in Q1 (prohibited AI practices), both the baseline and reranked methods cited page 51 of the EU AI Act, although the retrieved passage did not fully support the generated answer.

This demonstrates that a generated answer can appear credible while its citation is incomplete or incorrect.

### Incomplete or Misleading Answers

Another limitation appeared in Q5 (social scoring).

The baseline method correctly identified that certain social-scoring practices are prohibited but incorrectly described them as high-risk rather than prohibited practices.

Reranking improved the explanation, but this example demonstrates that retrieving relevant documents does not always guarantee a fully accurate answer.

### Potential Improvements

- Verify that each generated claim is supported by its cited document chunk.
- Improve citation handling for sources with missing page numbers or metadata.
- Use reranking to prioritize more relevant evidence.
- Evaluate the system using more questions and repeated tests.

**Conclusion:** Reranking can improve retrieval relevance and answer accuracy, but it does not completely prevent unsupported claims, incorrect citations, or incomplete answers.
